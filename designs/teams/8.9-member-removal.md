# Removing a team member

Suspension stops a member for now and lets them come back. Removal is for
good: the account goes, and with it everything that could act for them. The
work they did stays where it was, readable by the team's owners and admins,
and nothing is handed to anyone else.

## Who may remove whom

The rules are suspension's. An owner removes anyone but themselves; an admin
removes members only. Two people are always kept: the team's last active
owner, and the account a deployment names as its installation's own owner
(`OMNIGENT_INSTALLATION_OWNER`, for a deployment whose own pages are built
around one account). The change needs a person signed in through a browser,
like every other team change, and it is decided inside the write, against the
roles as they are then. `DELETE /auth/users/{id}` removes a team member the
same way, with the same rules.

## One transaction

`SqlAlchemyAccountStore.remove_member` does, in one transaction under the
team lock:

- the account deletion's tombstone: shares, schedules, device and refresh
  grants, hosts (cloud computers stay as tombstones for their provider),
  approval authority, preferences and tokens;
- the membership retired with its identity bindings, so a proxy sign-in for
  them is bound to nobody;
- the pending invitations they could still use (to one of their addresses,
  or linking their account) revoked; invitations they issued to other people
  stay theirs;
- the admission revision bumped, so a front proxy hears of it;
- a record: who removed them and when, the admission revision, the threads
  they owned and the cloud computers to remove
  (`team_member_removals`, `team_member_removal_items`).

A crash before the commit changes nothing. After it, every credential of
theirs is refused, because every request reads the tombstone.

## After the commit

Then, as a suspension does: cached sessions are dropped, open streams and
tunnels re-check their caller at once (`authority_changed`), and the
admission sync pushes now. Last, each cloud computer gets its provider's
`terminate`. Each step is safe to repeat and is retried on its own anyway:
the admission sync pushes at start and hourly, and the managed-sandbox reaper
retries a terminate its provider did not confirm. Removing the member again
runs them all once more.

## What the team page shows

`GET /v1/team` lists the latest removals, and
`GET /v1/team/members/{id}/removal` adds the threads they owned:

| Step | Done when |
| --- | --- |
| Access blocked | The transaction committed |
| Member group | The admission sync applied a revision at least the removal's (a proxy's adapter confirms only what it read back); `not_used` without one |
| Computers | Every recorded cloud computer's tombstone has no sandbox left: its provider accepted the removal. A link to the provider's fleet page (`PersistentHostPolicy.fleet_url`) where an owner or admin finishes deleting them |
| Devices | `not_used` until a deployment registers per-member devices (`DeviceRegistrations`) |

A step not done yet says so and offers Retry now, which repeats the removal.

## Not here

A removal that survives restoring the database to before it, copying a
removed member's work to someone else, retention windows and audit export.
Turns and approvals keep their recorded attribution, so the former member's
name stays on what they did.
